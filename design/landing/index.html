The problem

Good intentions get lost. And an estate can take a year to wind up.

When a parent dies, bank accounts freeze while the estate is reported, an executor is appointed and creditors are paid. In that gap, registration is blocked, rent falls behind and the family car is sold in a hurry for far less than it's worth.

  • 66%of South Africans don't have a will.
  • 2×A simple estate used to take 6–9 months to wind up. Now it takes about twice as long.
  • 2.8mSouth African children have lost a parent; 64% of them lost their father.

Sources: Sanlam Legacy Wills Survey 2025 via TimesLIVE (19 Sep 2025); Moneyweb via The Citizen (2 Dec 2024); South African Child Gauge 2024 via Cape Times (22 Aug 2024).

Trust and safety

The agent advises. The contract enforces. People you trust decide.

  • Limits no one can talk past

    Caps, approved institutions and the car's minimum price live in a Solana program. Not even the AI agent can move money outside them.

  • Two of three trusted people

    Your executor, a lawyer, a family elder. A passing is only accepted when two of them confirm it, and unusual requests need them too.

  • "I'm still here"

    A waiting period follows every report of a passing. One tap from you cancels it and locks the vault again.

  • Hardship, handled with care

    A sworn affidavit and two trusted people can let the car sell below your price, but never below your hardship floor.

  • Private papers stay private

    Death certificates and IDs never go on a public ledger. Only a fingerprint of each document does, so anyone can check nothing was altered.

  • Your keys, on your phone

    Wallets are created with Tether WDK and the recovery phrase stays on your device. Heirloom's servers never hold your keys.
